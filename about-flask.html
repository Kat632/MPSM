My Primary School is at the Museum

What if the museum
became the classroom?

MPSM explores what happens when children spend extended periods learning in museums, galleries and other cultural places, rather than simply visiting them for the day.

The MPSM pilot projects explored what happens when the museum becomes part of children's everyday learning.

A different kind
of school day.

Museums are usually places children visit. MPSM asks a different question: what if children stayed?

My Primary School is at the Museum was developed to explore what could happen when primary and nursery classes spend an extended period learning in a museum, gallery or other cultural setting.

The lessons don't stop. The curriculum doesn't disappear. What changes is the environment in which learning takes place.

Objects, collections, buildings, gardens, stories, people and places become part of the children's everyday experience. Teachers have new possibilities for approaching the curriculum, while children have the opportunity to explore, question and make connections in ways that are difficult to reproduce in a conventional classroom.

The idea behind MPSM

Why take children to a museum
for one day when they could
learn there every day?

That simple shift in thinking is at the heart of MPSM.

Where it began

An idea about
place and possibility.

The idea for MPSM grew from Wendy James's work with museums and cultural buildings and a growing realisation that these places contain extraordinary resources for learning.

A museum is not simply a building containing collections. It can be a place to explore science, history, art, language, mathematics, nature, engineering, citizenship and the world around us.

MPSM developed this idea into a practical experiment: what happens when a school and a cultural organisation work together to make that environment part of children's education for an extended period?

What did we discover?

Changing the place
can change the learning.

The research found benefits not just for children, but for teachers, museums and the wider relationship between schools and cultural organisations.

Children

Children developed confidence, communication, independence and new ways of engaging with culture and learning.

Teachers

Teachers found new freedom to experiment with creative approaches to the curriculum and learning outside the traditional classroom.

Museums

Museums gained new insight into younger audiences and new possibilities for using their spaces and collections.

Communities

Cultural organisations became more closely connected to children's everyday lives and their local communities.

More than a school trip

This isn't about
moving a lesson.

It's about changing the relationship between children, teachers and the places around them.

An MPSM residency is an extended partnership. Children spend enough time in a cultural setting for it to become familiar, for relationships to develop and for the place itself to become part of their learning.

That means asking different questions. What can this building teach us? What stories are hidden in this collection? Who works here? What can we investigate? What can we make? What can we discover that we could never discover in quite the same way at school?

The idea keeps growing

The museum is
the beginning.

MPSM has inspired projects in a wide range of cultural and natural settings. The approach has been adapted for museums and galleries, historic sites, gardens and other places where children can learn through direct experience.

Projects now extend across England, Wales, Scotland, the Republic of Ireland, Germany and Norway, with different partners adapting the idea to their own children, communities and collections.

Some projects have focused on early years education. Others have worked with primary pupils, specialist schools or children with additional learning needs. What they share is the belief that the environment around a child can be a powerful part of their education.
