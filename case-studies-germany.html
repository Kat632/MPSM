Germany, Case Study

Exploring a proposed museum-based learning group in Hamburg, bringing primary education and cultural learning together.

A Museum Class at Kiekeberg

A concept for an intensive educational learning group developed by ReBBZ Harburg and its cultural partners in Hamburg.

The proposal explores how a museum environment could support primary-age children whose emotional, social or educational needs make attendance in a conventional classroom difficult. It places cultural participation, practical learning and supportive relationships at the centre of the approach.

The proposal

A different setting for learning

The concept proposes a small learning group for children who need additional support, using museums and out-of-school learning to encourage confidence, curiosity, communication and a sense of belonging.

Learning through people, places and making

Aims of the Museum Class

The proposal places children's personal development alongside learning and cultural participation.

Confidence and identity

Create opportunities for children to strengthen their self-worth, develop a positive sense of identity and experience success.

Curiosity and creativity

Encourage exploration, practical activity, concentration and confidence in learning and making.

Communication

Support children to communicate with others, work alongside peers and develop relationships in a small-group setting.

Belonging and participation

Use shared experiences and cultural spaces to help children feel part of a group and connected to the wider community.

Learning beyond the classroom

The concept envisages a learning environment where children can explore real objects, places and processes through direct experience. At Kiekeberg, suggested activities include planting and watering, caring for animals, basket weaving and building with natural materials.

These activities were proposed as ways to combine practical learning with opportunities to develop confidence, cooperation and group relationships. The document sets out the intended approach rather than reporting measured outcomes.

The proposal

The Museum Class Concept

The concept proposes an intensive, small-group learning environment for primary-age children whose support needs make regular school attendance difficult or temporarily unworkable.

Why a museum?

The document argues that museums can offer more than collections and exhibitions. They can provide opportunities for exploration, hands-on activity and cultural participation, particularly for children who may have limited access to such experiences elsewhere.

The proposal draws on the idea of museum-school partnerships in the United Kingdom and considers how a similar approach could be developed in Hamburg.

Who is the proposal intended to support?

The intended group is children around 8 to 11 years old, broadly covering school years 3 to 5, who experience significant emotional, social or educational difficulties and need a more supportive learning environment.

The concept envisages a small group, with teaching and support shared between education professionals and appropriate social-care staff.

Intended aims

  • Strengthen self-worth and a positive sense of identity.
  • Help children experience self-efficacy and success.
  • Develop communication and relationships with others.
  • Encourage curiosity, creativity and concentration.
  • Build confidence in learning and practical work.
  • Support a sense of belonging within the group and wider society.

How the learning group was envisaged

The original sketch proposed a school-day timetable from 8am to 1pm, with the group meeting at ReBBZ Harburg before travelling together to the museum. Teaching was intended to involve the museum's education service, a special educator and other support staff as needed.

These details describe the plan set out in the source document. They should not be read as confirmation that the programme was subsequently implemented.

The proposed partnership

Kiekeberg Open-Air Museum

The Freilichtmuseum am Kiekeberg was identified in the concept as a potential first museum partner for the proposed learning group.

A practical learning environment

The museum's themes, including food and agriculture, natural science and regional history, were considered suitable for learning through direct experience. Its outdoor setting and opportunities to encounter animals and nature were seen as valuable features.

Space and routine

The proposal explored the use of a separate building or room, together with a cloakroom, to provide the group with a consistent base and help reduce distractions. Starting at Kiekeberg was intended to make the transition into a museum-based learning routine more manageable.

Learning by doing

Suggested activities included planting and watering, caring for animals, basket weaving and building with natural materials. The concept proposed that shared practical tasks could support both learning and the development of group relationships.

Proposed organisation and costs

The source document envisaged museum-based sessions on at least three days per week, with the museum's education service providing 1.5 hours of activity per day. The remaining provision was to involve school and support staff.

The original estimate for the museum costs was €100 per day for the group, including admission, room and site use, cleaning and electricity. It also described the possibility of additional days at €40 per day for admission and room or site use. The document estimated museum costs of €6,360 for the first half-year.

These are figures from the original proposal, not confirmation of actual expenditure or current prices.

A proposal for cultural participation

The Kiekeberg partnership was presented as one way to connect education, cultural participation and practical experience. The concept also identified other museums in the Harburg area as potential partners.
