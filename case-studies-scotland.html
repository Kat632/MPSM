The Schoolseum Project 2026 - Blackness Primary School

A week-long Schoolseum residency exploring fashion, textile design and the creative industries with Primary 7 learners from Blackness Primary School.

The Project

Becoming designers, makers and creative thinkers

As part of the Schoolseum programme at V&A Dundee, Primary 7 learners from Blackness Primary School participated in an intensive week-long residency exploring the world of fashion and textiles.

Through hands-on workshops, gallery exploration and engagement with creative practitioners, pupils followed the design process from initial inspiration through to finished creations.

The week culminated in a public fashion show on the V&A Dundee Plaza, where learners showcased their original designs.

Exploring Fashion and Design

Throughout the residency, pupils worked as designers, researchers, makers and presenters.

Gallery Exploration

Discovering inspiration through exhibitions, materials, pattern, colour and storytelling.

Creative Careers

Discovering the many creative and technical roles within fashion, textiles and design.

Design and Making

Developing concepts, experimenting with materials and creating original designs.

Presentation

Building confidence and communicating ideas through styling, movement and storytelling.

The Celebration

A Fashion Show on the Plaza

The residency culminated in a vibrant public fashion show on the V&A Dundee Plaza.

Each learner had the opportunity to present their original designs to families, teachers, museum visitors and invited guests.

The event celebrated the pupils' creativity, imagination and achievements, bringing their journey from initial ideas to finished designs to a public stage.

Outcomes and Impact

The residency provided opportunities for pupils to develop creative skills, confidence and understanding of the wider design industry.

Creative Career Awareness

Discovering the breadth of opportunities within fashion and the creative industries.

Creativity and Design Skills

Developing visual communication, creative thinking and problem-solving.

Confidence

Building confidence in sharing ideas and presenting work to an audience.

Connections to Culture and Design

Engaging directly with Scotland's design museum and discovering how creativity shapes everyday life.

Pride and Ownership

Experiencing the achievement of creating original work and sharing it publicly.

Inside the Schoolseum Project

A museum as a place for learning

Schoolseum was developed at V&A Dundee, Scotland's first dedicated design museum. The project drew on Wendy James' My Primary School is at the Museum concept and explored how cultural settings can support learning, creativity and career awareness.

The school partner was Rowantree Primary School in Dundee, described in the report as the highest-ranked school in the Scottish Index of Multiple Deprivation (SIMD).

Exploring creative disciplines

Pupils worked across four areas: jewellery and fashion design, architecture and engineering, game design, and curation and exhibition design. These activities offered opportunities to explore ideas, experiment, solve problems and communicate their thinking.

Learning beyond the museum

The project also included visits to other Dundee cultural and educational organisations:

  • McManus Galleries
  • RSS Discovery
  • HMS Unicorn
  • Dundee Science Centre

The project concluded with a showcase day for families, educators and the wider community. The report noted that many families had not visited a museum before.

Learning and Impact

What Did the Evaluation Find?

Assessing pupils' development

Student self-assessments used the Stellar Assessment Tool to examine areas including research, fixing and failing, reflection, concept development and communication.

Measured improvements

  • 85% of students improved in at least two assessed areas.
  • 82% increased their overall score.
  • Communication scores rose by 519 points, representing an average increase of 16%.

Individual progress

The report highlighted Student B as the largest individual improvement, with a score increasing from 20 to 89.

Looking ahead

Teachers observed increased curiosity among pupils. The project also attracted interest from Education Scotland, with efforts made to secure funding for future development.

These findings offer an encouraging example of how creative, museum-based learning can support children's development and connect classroom learning with cultural experiences.

Inside the Blackness Primary School Residency

Project Aims

The week-long residency aimed to immerse young people in the world of design while providing meaningful insight into the wide range of creative careers connected to fashion and textiles.

The project sought to:

  • Increase awareness of creative careers within fashion, textiles and design.
  • Develop creativity, confidence and communication skills.
  • Enable pupils to work in a professional cultural environment.
  • Build understanding of the design process from concept development to final presentation.
  • Encourage teamwork, problem-solving and independent thinking.
  • Provide an authentic platform for pupils to celebrate and share their work.

Gallery Exploration and Inspiration

Learners explored V&A Dundee's exhibitions and displays, examining how designers draw inspiration from culture, history, materials and contemporary issues.

Pupils investigated pattern, colour, texture and storytelling through design, using the museum environment as a starting point for their own creative ideas.

Creative Career Insights

The residency introduced pupils to the wide range of careers associated with fashion and textiles, helping them understand that successful fashion projects rely on many different creative and technical roles.

  • Fashion Designer
  • Textile Designer
  • Costume Designer
  • Pattern Cutter
  • Stylist
  • Fashion Illustrator
  • Marketing and Communications Professional
  • Curator
  • Exhibition Designer
  • Museum Learning Practitioner

Design Development

Working individually and collaboratively, pupils developed their own fashion concepts. They produced sketches, explored materials, experimented with colour palettes and refined their ideas through discussion and feedback.

Making and Prototyping

Learners translated their ideas into physical outcomes, creating wearable pieces and textile-based designs.

Pupils were encouraged to test ideas, solve problems and adapt their work in response to challenges encountered during the making process.

Presentation and Performance

The final stage focused on presentation skills and confidence building. Learners prepared for a professional-style runway event, considering how fashion is communicated to an audience through movement, styling and storytelling.

Fashion Show on the Plaza

The project culminated in a vibrant public fashion show on the V&A Dundee Plaza. This event provided an authentic audience and a memorable platform for pupils to showcase their creativity.

Each learner had the opportunity to present their work publicly, celebrating the learning, effort and imagination invested throughout the week. Families, school staff, museum visitors and supporters witnessed the transformation of initial ideas into finished designs.

The fashion show created a powerful sense of achievement and demonstrated the quality of work produced by the young people during the residency.

Outcomes and Impact

Increased Awareness of Creative Careers

Pupils developed a greater understanding of the breadth of opportunities available within the creative industries. Many learners reported discovering careers they had not previously considered.

Enhanced Creativity and Design Skills

Through iterative design processes, pupils demonstrated growth in visual communication, creative thinking and problem-solving abilities.

Improved Confidence

Presenting work in a public setting helped learners build confidence in sharing ideas, discussing their creative choices and performing in front of an audience.

Stronger Connections to Culture and Design

Working within Scotland's design museum enabled pupils to engage directly with world-class design and understand how creativity shapes everyday life.

Sense of Pride and Ownership

The opportunity to create and publicly showcase original work generated a strong sense of accomplishment among participants.

Learner Voice

Throughout the residency, pupils demonstrated enthusiasm, curiosity and resilience. Learners embraced challenges, supported one another and grew increasingly confident in discussing their creative decisions.

Many pupils highlighted the excitement of working in the museum environment and valued the opportunity to experience design in a real-world context rather than solely within the classroom.

Teacher Reflections

School staff observed high levels of engagement throughout the week and noted positive developments in learner confidence, communication, collaboration and creative thinking.

The residency provided opportunities for pupils to excel in different ways, allowing learners with diverse strengths to contribute meaningfully to the project.

Conclusion

The Blackness Primary School Schoolseum residency successfully combined design education, creative career exploration and authentic real-world experiences.

By embedding learners within V&A Dundee for a week, the project enabled young people to gain valuable insight into fashion and textile careers while developing transferable skills that will support future learning and employment pathways.

The final fashion show on the Plaza served as a celebration of creativity, self-expression and achievement, demonstrating the transformative potential of cultural learning partnerships between schools and museums.

Through Schoolseum, learners were not simply visitors to the museum; they became designers, makers and creative thinkers, showcasing their talents on a public stage and gaining confidence in their ability to shape the future through design.

Transforming education through long-term partnerships between primary schools and cultural organisations.
