The Potential of Extended Cultural Residencies for Young Children

About this publication

This research explores the potential learning benefits of extended cultural residencies for young children, examining two museum-school partnerships involving children aged three to five.

Drawing on qualitative research and case studies, the paper considers how sustained experiences in museum settings can support children's language and communication skills, as well as their personal, social and emotional development.

Jennifer DeWitt, Heather King, Denise Wright & Kate Measures · 2018

Museum Management and Curatorship, 33(2), 158–177.

About the authors

Jennifer DeWitt – UCL Institute of Education.

Jennifer DeWitt is a research and evaluation consultant and Senior Research Fellow at UCL Institute of Education. Her research focuses on learning and engagement in informal science environments, including museums, families and school groups. She has extensive experience researching science capital and equity in science education.

Heather King – King's College London.

Heather King is a Professor in Science Education at King's College London. Her research examines how educators foster learner engagement with science across schools, museums, the natural environment and other informal learning spaces. Her work also explores social and environmental justice in education.

Denise Wright – Edge Hill University.

Denise Wright is a consultant, educator and visual artist with more than 20 years' experience. Her work includes creative projects in community settings, prisons and art galleries. She has also taught in higher education, specialising in early years, child development, learning through play and educational inclusion.

University of Cambridge Museums Nursery in Residence Project

About this publication

This report documents the University of Cambridge Museums Nursery in Residence project, exploring how an extended partnership between a nursery and a museum can support young children's learning and development.

Using a practitioner-led case study and action research, the report highlights reflective practice and the value of evidence-based collaboration across cultural and educational settings.

Nicola Wallis & Kate Noble · 2018

About the authors

Nicola Wallis – Early Years Programme Manager, National Portrait Gallery.

Nicola Wallis specialises in early years education and museum learning, with a particular interest in arts and cultural education, young children's experiences in museums and participatory research. She worked on the University of Cambridge Museums' Nursery in Residence project, exploring how young children make meaning through sustained engagement with museums and gardens. She now works as Early Years Programme Manager at the National Portrait Gallery.

Kate Noble – Assistant Research Professor, Museum Participation and Practice, Fitzwilliam Museum, University of Cambridge.

Kate Noble's research explores creative, collections-based learning and how museums can support imagination, dialogue and collaboration. She has led numerous research and evaluation projects examining children's learning, participation and engagement with museum collections.
